So, How Do You Maintain a Stable Coding Mood?

May 23, 2026

Today I will try to give you a few ideas that might help ease the burden of your life as a programmer.

A few days ago I saw a programmer's story on Facebook, which I think all of you have experienced the same thing or are even experiencing right now.

So the story goes something like this:

“

How do you keep your coding mood from fluctuating? Sometimes you're enthusiastic, sometimes you're bored, and your brain is stuck.

This may be a common thing for you to feel, but we must not be silent, because sometimes this unstable work mood will affect our work performance, what's more, unstable industrial conditions can be because you are unable to maintain your coding mood, which is one of the reasons you get laid off, I pray this will never happen to you.

The good news is, I have experienced, I was once a victim of my own unstable mood.

Reason

Let's start by finding out what causes this unstable mood. There's an interesting quote that I really like.

“

Knowing the cause of a problem is half the solution.

Just like a bug, it wouldn't occur without a cause. And the good news is, the bug occurred because our app was being used. If our app is being used, it means it's useful; we're making a useful app, right?

We can take a little positive thing here, if you experience an unstable coding mood, it's a sign that you're coding, which is better than being a useless person lying around. Give yourself some credit for choosing not to be a lazy person.

Let's return to the topic of finding the cause of this problem. Based on my experience over the past eight years as a programmer, there are roughly causes of this unstable mood.

  1. Stress - This usually happens when you want to code something you've never done before.
  2. Lack of sleep - Trigger a weakened immune system, impaired concentration, memory decline, and emotional instability.
  3. Procrastinating on work - The habit of putting off work is often referred to as procrastination. This condition is not a sign of laziness, but rather a self-protective response by the brain to avoid negative emotions or feelings—such as boredom, anxiety, or the fear of failure—associated with the task.
  4. Health - When your health isn't good, it is extremely difficult to stay focused and maintain a good mood.

So, can you picture it? Once we know the source of a problem, our brains automatically figure out the answers to those issues without even being told.

Keeping the Spirit Alive

Maintaining one's enthusiasm is extremely difficult, as we often derive that drive from external sources—such as the prospect of a salary raise, travel opportunities, and various other things.

While all of that is positive, we also need to consider how to maintain our motivation independently. When it comes to motivation, there are a few questions we need to ask ourselves.

  1. What is our purpose in life? - I believe that to navigate life, we need passion and aspirations. So, ask yourself what you want to achieve in this career, and then create a roadmap to reach that goal.
  2. What are you like? - Figure out what you enjoy and what suits you best. Do you prefer a procedural, object-oriented, or functional coding style? Make yourself as comfortable as possible and try out different things; eventually, you will discover what works best for you.
  3. Have you been of benefit to others? - As a programmer, you spend a great deal of time learning; have you shared any of that knowledge with others? Have you set aside a portion of your earnings for those less fortunate than yourself?
  4. Who are you friends with? - There are people who uplift your spirits when you talk to them, and others who do the opposite. Therefore, be wise in choosing your friends; it is best to befriend those who inspire you—people who are smarter and more experienced—and to learn as much as you can from them.

For me, maintaining that spirit is largely about purifying the heart. Be the person who understands yourself best, and be honest with both yourself and others. Surround yourself with a good, supportive environment. I am confident this will go a long way in sustaining your motivation.

Don't be afraid to dream big. But remember, those dreams don't have to be realized immediately. What matters most is taking it step by step and being able to enjoy the process, the struggle, and—ultimately—the fruits of your hard work.

“

Resolve things within yourself so that you can focus.

Learn, Learn, and Learn

In any field—not just for programmers—learning is essential. Even construction work involves specific knowledge. The same applies to programmers; our lives become easier as our knowledge grows.

Don't be afraid to delve deeper into a technology—for instance, by learning how to create a database. Doing so will give you a better understanding of databases, so that if you later encounter issues with the one you are using, you won't struggle to pinpoint the source of the problem.

Having a deep understanding of a technology's concepts helps you determine the best way to use it. Since you are aware of its strengths and weaknesses, you can select the most suitable technology for the project you are working on.

One of the most important aspects of learning is keeping a record of what you have studied. After all, I am certain you will forget—we all forget things quite often. By taking notes while you learn, you are essentially creating a backup of your brain.

Side Project

Have you ever felt bored with your current office job? Or do you feel like you don't quite understand much of the technology used at your office? Or perhaps you feel like your work lacks sufficient challenge?

If the answer is "yes," then you absolutely must start a personal project. If you are wondering how to begin a personal project, try doing this.

  1. Create tools for your own use - You are a programmer who creates software to solve people's problems; you possess a skill that most people lack, so treat yourself to software you've built yourself to solve your own problems.
  2. Create a mini-project using the technologies used at your office - When working in a team, you will often deal with code you didn't write yourself; you can use side projects as a way to learn and understand the code, frameworks, libraries, or technologies employed by your teammates. This is incredibly beneficial: the more you understand code written by others, the easier it will be to fix it should any issues arise later.
  3. An interesting clone project - Sometimes you need software available on the market but don't use it very often—yet still require it; this is a great reason to build a side project. You might not build it perfectly, and you don't have to; you will gain a wealth of knowledge along the way, and as a bonus, you can customize the software to suit your specific needs.
  4. Trying out new technology - If you are looking for a challenge in your daily coding activities, you could try this idea, as new technologies often bring very interesting challenges.

I frequently encourage my friends to start their own personal projects—whatever those might be—because, based on my own experience, I have gained so much from them. The benefits range from job and business offers to acquiring new knowledge, and more.

Having a side project feels a lot like having a child. Sometimes, when we spend too much time coding at the behest of a boss or supervisor, we grow weary of writing code based on someone else's direction. A side project offers you the chance to write code exactly the way you want.

Make time to improve the code

Many bugs arise from decisions that weren't fully thought through. So, give yourself time to consider how to improve your code and find the best way to solve the problem you're currently facing.

To make your code easier to maintain in the long run, document it using clear and simple language so that others will have no trouble understanding what you have written.

Avoid excessive abstraction in the code you write; if you use a library, ensure you understand how it works, as well as its strengths and limitations. Do not be afraid to write your own version of the code, as doing so helps you understand how to solve problems through your own implementation.

Take the time to read the documentation thoroughly and ensure you understand the basic concepts. Don't be afraid to practice writing your own version.

Make it a habit to regularly check your own code, as reviewing it yourself can reveal potential bugs that went unnoticed while you were writing it.

Sometimes, we just need to spend a little more time making our lives easier in the long run.

conclusion

Maintaining a stable coding mood is not about waiting for motivation to appear, but about building healthy habits, clear goals, and continuous learning. Mood fluctuations are normal for programmers, but understanding their causes—such as stress, lack of sleep, procrastination, and poor health—helps us manage them effectively.

To stay motivated, programmers should have a clear purpose, understand their preferred way of working, contribute to others, and surround themselves with supportive people. Continuous learning, taking notes, and deeply understanding technologies are essential for long-term growth.

Successful programmer maintains both technical skills and personal well-being. By balancing learning, health, motivation, and consistent practice, it becomes much easier to stay productive and enjoy a long, sustainable career in programming.
